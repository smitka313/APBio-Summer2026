Investigation 1 - Are These Results Reliable?

Watch Standard Deviation Help Video

Why standard deviation? Biologists use standard deviation to measure how much data vary from the mean. It helps determine whether results are consistent and reliable.
TermMeaning
MeanThe average value of a data set.
Standard DeviationA measure of how spread out data are from the mean.
VariabilityNatural differences found in biological data.

You are not expected to memorize this formula. Focus on what the calculations tell scientists.

How to Work Through the Calculation

  1. Add all data values together.
  2. Divide by the number of data points to calculate the mean.
  3. Subtract the mean from each data point.
  4. Square each difference.
  5. Add all squared differences together.
  6. Calculate the standard deviation.

Investigation 2 - Do These Groups Appear Different?

Watch SEM and Error Bars Help Video

SEM: The Standard Error of the Mean measures how precisely a sample mean estimates the true population mean. Error bars help compare groups visually.
Need help with SEM and error bars?
  • Watch the SEM and error bars support video linked above.
  • Calculate the mean and standard deviation before calculating SEM.
  • Remember: smaller error bars usually suggest a more precise estimate of the mean.
  • When error bars overlap, the difference between groups may not be significant.

Why chi-square? Biologists use chi-square to determine whether differences between observed and expected data are likely due to chance or may be biologically meaningful.
Need help with chi-square?
  • Watch the null hypothesis and chi-square support video linked above.
  • Start by calculating the expected values.
  • Use the chi-square table only after you calculate your chi-square value and degrees of freedom.
  • Your final conclusion should explain whether the observed data are significantly different from the expected data.

How to Work Through the Calculation

  1. Calculate expected values.
  2. Subtract expected values from observed values.
  3. Square each difference.
  4. Divide each squared difference by the expected value.
  5. Add values to calculate chi-square.
  6. Determine degrees of freedom.
  7. Compare your chi-square value to the critical value table.
  8. Reject or fail to reject the null hypothesis.

Common Photo Investigation Mistakes

Weak Explanation

“This is a flower.”

This only identifies the object. It does not explain the biology.

Strong Explanation

“This flower may demonstrate mutualism because it attracts pollinators that help transfer pollen while obtaining nectar.”

This connects the photo to a biological interaction and explains the process.

🧪 Assignment #3 - AP Biology Investigation Design

Design a biological investigation based on a real-world observation. You do not need to conduct the experiment.

Length: approximately 1–2 pages plus a data table.
Submit: A typed investigation design in Google Docs or Microsoft Word.

Choose a Biological Observation

Possible topics include plant growth, enzyme activity, fermentation, photosynthesis, osmosis, decomposition, transpiration, bacterial growth, or biodiversity.

Required Sections

1. Research Question

Write one clear, testable question. Strong example: How does temperature affect the rate of yeast respiration?

2. Background Information

Write one paragraph explaining the biological concept, why it matters, and what biology predicts.

3. Hypothesis

Use if-then-because: If _____, then _____ because _____.

4. Variables and Control

Identify IV, DV, at least three controlled variables, and a control group.

5. Experimental Procedure

Write a repeatable numbered procedure with sample size/trials and measurement plan.

6. Data Table and Graph Plan

Create one blank data table and describe the graph, x-axis, and y-axis.

7. Predicted Results

Explain what you think will happen and why using biological reasoning.

8. Limitations and Sources of Error

Identify at least two possible limitations or errors and explain their effect.

9. AP Biology Connection

Identify one Big Idea and one Science Practice and explain the connection.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I use my phone for photos?

Yes. Phone photos are completely fine as long as they are original and include your proof object.

Can I use a pet in a photo?

Yes. Pets can be used when they clearly connect to a biological concept, such as phenotype, behavior, adaptation, or artificial selection.

Can I use family members?

Occasionally, but most photos should come from natural or biological examples around you. Do not make all of your examples human-centered.

Can one photo represent multiple concepts?

No. Each of your 15 photos should represent a different AP Biology concept.

What if I lose my packet?

Use this website as a reference and complete your work on separate sheets of paper. Submit those papers with your summer assignment.

Can I use AI?

You may use AI or online tools to help understand concepts, but all submitted explanations must be written in your own words. Do not submit AI-generated writing as your own.
